Who we are

A foundation of engineers, scientists and makers putting technology to work for the living world.

Common Ground is a Dutch stichting founded by three people from technical backgrounds — robotics, AI and data science, drones, photography and film. It grows out of doctoral research into robotics for wildlife conservation: a home where projects that begin in academia can continue, independently and on their own terms, long after. We build practical tools, gather hard data, and put both in service of protecting nature.

What we do

Technology, pointed at nature

We pair field-ready hardware with rigorous data science — the same toolkit that stresses ecosystems, turned toward understanding and defending them.

Autonomous monitoring

Walking and rolling robots that survey habitats where people can't easily go, gathering data hour after hour.

Spatial data & GIS

Open-data mapping of pressure, habitat and connectivity — turning diffuse worry into a ranked, spatial to-do list.

Aerial & drone survey

Low-cost aerial imaging for censuses, change detection and reaching terrain that ground teams cannot.

AI & data science

Detection, classification and analysis that finds the signal — which species, which places, which trends matter most.

Photography & film

Documentary imagery that makes the science legible and the stakes felt — because protection needs an audience.

Fieldwork & partnerships

Working alongside researchers and reserves on the ground, where tools have to earn their keep in the real world.

Research · case study

Endangered: mapping human pressure on Kenya's wildlife

The first output of Common Ground Research — a reproducible, open-data screen of where human activity most threatens the country's flagship reserves.

Open data · fully reproducible

We built a Human Pressure Index across Kenya from free global data — settlement, roads and built-up land — then measured the pressure pressing in on 14 flagship protected areas and along the corridors between them.

The pattern is stark: the most besieged refuges are the small, accessible ones, while size and remoteness buy breathing room. It's a map of exactly where autonomous monitoring earns its keep — high conflict, thin data.
